Current Research

Bayes-Optimal Learning and Generative Modeling

Bayes Optimal Learning Threshold (BOLT) introduces a novel approach to design loss function: it derives a parameterized upper bound on the Bayes error rate and using its minimization to guide model training toward Bayes-optimal performance. Originally developed for classification, the BOLT framework has been extended to generative adversarial networks (BOLT-GAN). BOLT shows improved training stability and generative performance. Its underlying principle provides a general foundation for designing learning objectives across different learning problems. The original BOLT work received the Best Student Paper Award at IEEE ICASSP 2025.

Communication-Efficient Distributed Learning

Communication overhead is a major bottleneck in distributed learning, particularly over wireless networks. Our research addresses this challenge through the development of communication-efficient techniques, including gradient sparsification, device scheduling, and over-the-air aggregation. Beyond reducing communication costs, we investigate the fundamental role of aggregation errors in distributed learning. In particular, our Global Unknown Estimation framework interprets distributed aggregation as a statistical estimation problem, highlighting that conventional computation-error metrics do not necessarily reflect the impact of aggregation on learning performance. Instead, the bias and variance of aggregation errors provide a more meaningful characterization, motivating new approaches to the design and optimization of distributed learning systems.

Intelligent Communications and Sensing Systems

Many problems in signal processing share mathematical structures with those encountered in machine learning, creating opportunities to leverage learning-inspired techniques for efficient system design and optimization. Our research explores these connections to address various challenges in wireless communications and sensing, including array design, precoding, resource allocation, and object detection. UUtilizing machine learning techniques along with model-based signal processing, we develop computationally-efficient approaches to address key design problems in these domains.

Learning-Based Optimization and Decision-Making

Recent advances in machine learning have opened new opportunities for addressing complex problems in prediction, optimization, and decision-making. Our research investigates how modern learning paradigms, including foundation models, generative modeling, and reinforcement learning, can be leveraged to address such challenges. We are particularly interested in understanding the capabilities and limitations of these approaches and developing methods that improve their effectiveness, reliability, and adaptability in real-world applications, with a particular emphasis on finance and automation.
